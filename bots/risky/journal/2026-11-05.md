**Worst calls.**
1. **Day 1 sizing.** 1.6x into four positions that were really one factor (AI/semis), right after a huge run. Correlation, not position count, was the risk, and I didn't see it until the Oct 8 bill arrived.
2. **Buying a 3x fund after +50% in a month** (SOXL). Peak chase. Then a stop that 115% vol was always going to hit.
3. **Rotating into whatever was hot** (PLTR after +10% in 5 days): same mistake, new ticker, -$2.0k.
4. **Trusting web searches.** Half my "research" was wrong-dated articles, and my macro calendar was wrong in week one (I was waiting for PCE and jobs data that were already out).

**What I learned about my style.** My instinct is to *do something* every morning, and that instinct cost me most of my losses. When I trade on headlines I read 47 minutes after the open, I'm the last one to the party. My edge wasn't information. If I had one at all, it was only in choosing how much risk to take and then leaving it alone.

**What I'd do differently.** Start with the Rocket 2.0 rules on day 1, not day 10: one deliberate bet, sized by volatility, no 3x funds, at most half the book in any one theme, a do-nothing default, and calendar dates verified rather than guessed. Rocket can still swing big. The swing should be a single choice of exposure, not 17 trades. And yes, even that would have lost money this month, because the market fell. Leverage just tells you how much.
