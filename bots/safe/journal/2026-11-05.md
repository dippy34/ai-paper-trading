## 🏁 Final reflection
**Result:** about +2.0% for the month with a maximum drawdown from peak under 1%, against the S&P 500 at roughly -2.6% over the same stretch. That's a positive month with a smooth ride, which is exactly what the turtle signed up for.

**Best calls**
- **Leaning into defensives in a hiking cycle.** PG, BRK-B, XLV and KO all gained 13–16%. With the Fed hiking, 10-year yields at 24-year highs and oil over $100, staples and healthcare were the place to be, and they carried the whole account.
- **Avoiding long bonds and utilities** on day one. Rates kept rising.
- **Scaling in slowly** and keeping 20–35% in cash and T-bills, which let me sleep through every wobble.
- **XLF**: buying financials when "higher for longer" was the theme, +5%.

**Worst calls**
- **The QQQ add.** When asked for a bit more return, I bought tech on a dip, and the dip became a -12.8% month for the Nasdaq. Total cost about $670. The sizing was sensible, but I bought falling momentum and called it value.
- **VOO bought near the highs** in early October. The index ended down, so the core position lost about 3%.
- **A calendar mistake** in week one (I misdated the jobs report). It was harmless, but it was sloppy.

**What I learned about my style**
- My edge is not losing. When I trusted the boring stuff, it paid. When I reached for return, the market charged me for it.
- Rule-based exits work. The -7% rule and the tripwires made the hard calls easy and kept me from hoping.
- My web research tool was nearly useless for same-day news, so price action and simple rules did most of the work.
- In this engine T-bill ETFs drop on ex-dividend dates and dividends aren't credited, so plain cash is just as good here.

**What I'd do differently**
- When asked for more return, I'd add more of what was already working (staples, healthcare, financials) or broaden with an equal-weight or value tilt, not buy the weakest sector.
- I'd keep a little more dry powder for genuine pullbacks instead of buying VOO in three quick tranches near a record.
